Science Gallery Melbourne Schools Program Responding to SENSUAL

Sonic Sense

Sonic Sense invites students to rethink what music can be and how it can be experienced. Inspired by SENSUAL, students collect sounds from the gallery and unexpected objects, experiment with Ableton and Max/MSP, and explore how our senses and brains shape perception. They then design an experimental composition for a particular person in mind, considering how their sensory experiences might influence the way they experience and interpret music.

Double module · 2 × 75-minute blocks · Levels 9–10, adaptable for 7–8 and VCE

01 — Student experience

What students will do

Across the two blocks, students move from listening to building — treating every step as both a creative and an investigative act.

05

Design for a user with different sensory needs

Perception isn't fixed — it's built moment to moment from whatever a person's senses hand the brain, and it plays out differently from one person to the next. Students use this as their creative material: not a problem to solve, but a wide field of listening possibilities to design into.

02 — Session structure

Two 75-minute blocks

BLOCK ONE — LISTEN & COLLECT
  • Respond to SENSUAL. Walk the current exhibition and note which works are heard, felt or otherwise sensed rather than just seen.
  • Sample the exhibits. Contact-mic a kinetic or tactile installation, capture the ambient hum of an immersive environment, record interactive elements as visitors trigger them.
  • Sample the everyday. Bring the same recording approach to objects in a bag — metal, glass, fabric, the body itself.
  • Talk perception. A short discussion on how people differ — what's louder, softer, missing or added depending on who's listening.
BLOCK TWO — BUILD & DESIGN
  • Build in Ableton. Arrange, warp and process the sample library from Block One.
  • Patch in Max for Live. Add one generative or processing device into the signal chain.
  • Name a person. Choose one real person and note what matters about how they experience sound.
  • Share and reflect. Play the piece, talk through the choices made for that person, and note what they noticed.
03 — Technology required

Software & hardware

SOFTWARE
  • Ableton Live — the core DAW for arranging, warping and processing samples.
  • Max for Live — patching inside Ableton for generative and custom processing devices.

This is the current software kit. Everything in the two blocks is scoped to run inside Ableton and Max for Live — no other packages required.

HARDWARE
  • Laptops running Ableton Live, one between two students
  • Closed-back headphones, one pair per student
  • Handheld recorders or phones for the gallery recording session
  • Contact microphones for capturing vibration inside objects and installations
  • Audio interface for mic input and low-latency monitoring
OPTIONAL — HAPTIC ELEMENTS

Where available, wearable haptics let a composition be felt as well as heard. A bass or percussive send from Ableton can drive a device like a bhaptics TactSuit, Woojer Strap or SubPac directly, so vibration becomes another instrument in the mix rather than a separate build.
